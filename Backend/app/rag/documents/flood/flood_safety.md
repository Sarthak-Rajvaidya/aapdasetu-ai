# Flood Safety

## Understanding warnings
- A **flood watch** means conditions are favorable for flooding — this is
  the time to prepare.
- A **flood warning** means flooding is happening or imminent — this is
  the time to act, including evacuating if advised.

## Before flooding
- Know whether your home is in a flood-prone or low-lying area.
- Prepare an emergency kit and keep important documents in a waterproof
  container.
- Identify at least two evacuation routes in case one becomes blocked.
- Move valuables and electrical items to higher levels of your home if
  time allows.

## During flooding
- Evacuate early rather than waiting for water to reach your home,
  especially if authorities advise it.
- **Never drive through flooded roads.** As little as 30 cm (about a
  foot) of moving water can sweep away a car, and it is impossible to
  judge road conditions or depth under water.
- **Never walk through moving floodwater.** It can hide strong currents,
  open manholes, debris, or electrical hazards.
- If trapped by rising water, move to the highest available level and
  signal for help; avoid closed attics without an escape route.
- Turn off electricity at the main switch if it is safe to do so and you
  can reach it without stepping in water.
- Assist elderly, disabled, or otherwise vulnerable neighbours if it is
  safe to do so, or alert responders to their location.

## After flooding
- Do not return home until authorities issue an all-clear.
- Avoid contact with floodwater, which may be contaminated; wash
  thoroughly if exposed.
- Watch for structural damage, washed-out roads, and downed power lines
  before re-entering buildings.
- Have electrical and gas systems checked by a professional before use if
  your home was flooded.

This is general educational guidance. Always follow instructions from
local disaster management authorities during an actual flood event.
