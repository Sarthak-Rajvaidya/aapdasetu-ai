# Wildfire Safety

## Before wildfire season / a warning
- Create "defensible space" around your home: clear dry leaves, brush, and
  flammable material within a few metres of the structure.
- Prepare an emergency kit and a "go bag" with essentials that you can
  grab in minutes.
- Plan at least two evacuation routes in case one is blocked by fire or
  smoke.
- Sign up for local emergency alerts if available.

## When a wildfire is approaching
- Monitor official updates closely; conditions can change very quickly
  with wind shifts.
- Do not wait for a mandatory evacuation order if you feel unsafe — leave
  early, especially if you have vulnerable family members, pets, or
  limited mobility in the household.
- Pack your vehicle and keep it facing the direction of your evacuation
  route.
- Wear long sleeves and closed shoes for protection from embers if you
  must be outside.

## Evacuating
- Follow official evacuation routes and instructions, even if they are
  longer than a route you would normally take, since blocked or smoky
  roads are dangerous.
- Keep vehicle windows closed and air recirculating to reduce smoke
  exposure.
- Never attempt to drive through heavy smoke with poor visibility.

## Important: do not fight the fire yourself
- Untrained residents should not attempt to fight a spreading wildfire.
  Firefighting requires specialized training and equipment; focus on
  evacuation and safety instead.

## After a wildfire
- Do not return home until officials confirm it is safe — hot spots and
  unstable structures can remain dangerous for a long time.
- Check for smouldering material near your property once you are cleared
  to return.

This is general educational guidance for preparedness and evacuation. In
an active wildfire, always follow instructions from local fire and
disaster authorities.
