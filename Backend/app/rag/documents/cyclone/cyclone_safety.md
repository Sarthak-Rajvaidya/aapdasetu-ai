
# Cyclone Safety and Preparedness Guide
## AapdaSetu AI — India-focused learning reference

**Purpose:** Help individuals and families understand cyclone hazards, prepare in advance, respond to official warnings, evacuate safely, and avoid secondary dangers after a cyclone.

**Important:** This guide is for education and preparedness. It is not a live warning service. During an actual emergency, follow the latest instructions from local authorities, the India Meteorological Department (IMD), and the relevant State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA).

---

## 1. What Is a Cyclone?

A tropical cyclone is a large rotating storm system that develops over warm ocean water. It can bring powerful winds, intense rainfall, rough seas, coastal flooding, and damage far inland.

In the North Indian Ocean, including the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea, these storms can affect coastal communities, islands, ports, fishing communities, and inland areas exposed to heavy rain and flooding.

### Main cyclone hazards

**Strong winds:** Can damage roofs, break branches, topple trees, damage buildings, and bring down power lines.

**Heavy rainfall:** Can cause river flooding, urban waterlogging, landslides, and disruption to roads and transport.

**Storm surge:** An abnormal rise in seawater pushed towards the coast by a storm. It can rapidly inundate low-lying coastal areas and is especially dangerous when combined with high tide.

**High waves and rough seas:** Create risks for fishing vessels, boats, coastal activities, and people standing near beaches, seawalls, or rocky shorelines.

**Secondary hazards:** These include electrical hazards, contaminated drinking water, damaged buildings, blocked roads, communication failures, and interruptions to healthcare.

A cyclone does not need to pass directly over a location to cause dangerous rain, flooding, or strong winds there.

## 2. Understanding Warnings and Forecasts

Cyclone forecasts can change as new observations become available. A forecast track is an estimate, not a guarantee that the storm will follow an exact path.

### Reliable information sources

- **India Meteorological Department (IMD):** Cyclone forecasts, warning bulletins, rainfall information, and official meteorological updates.
- **NDMA SACHET:** Official disaster alerts and preparedness guidance.
- **State and district authorities:** Local evacuation orders, shelter information, road restrictions, and area-specific safety instructions.
- **Local emergency services:** Assistance for urgent threats and emergencies.

Official resources:

- IMD cyclone information: https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/cyclone.php
- NDMA SACHET alerts and preparedness: https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/DosDont
- NDMA cyclone preparedness video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B9qR2e3xyJo
- NDMA early-warning and response video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jXEDxQzVFSs

### How to respond to warnings

1. Read the warning carefully and check the areas and time period it covers.
2. Identify whether your home, workplace, school, or travel route is in an affected area.
3. Prepare early rather than waiting for visible signs of the storm.
4. Follow evacuation instructions promptly.
5. Recheck official updates because conditions and instructions can change.
6. Do not forward unverified messages, old videos, or rumours as current emergency information.

Do not judge safety only by looking outside. Dangerous conditions can develop before the strongest winds or heaviest rain arrive.

## 3. Preparing Before a Cyclone

Preparation is most effective when completed before warnings become urgent.

### Make a household emergency plan

- Identify the safest nearby shelter and the official evacuation route.
- Decide where family members will meet if separated.
- Choose an out-of-area contact if mobile networks in your locality become unreliable.
- Make arrangements for children, older adults, people with disabilities, and anyone who needs medical assistance.
- Identify how pets and livestock can be moved or protected safely.
- Keep important emergency phone numbers written on paper.

### Prepare an emergency kit

Pack supplies in a sturdy, waterproof bag or container.

Recommended items include:

- Drinking water and ready-to-eat food.
- Essential medicines and copies of prescriptions.
- First-aid supplies.
- Torch or flashlight and spare batteries.
- Charged mobile phone, power bank, and charging cables.
- Battery-powered or hand-crank radio if available.
- Identification documents and important records stored in waterproof packaging.
- Some cash, hygiene products, masks, and necessary baby-care supplies.
- Clothes, sturdy footwear, and a whistle.
- Drinking-water containers and basic sanitation supplies.

Plan supplies around the needs of everyone in the household and the likely duration of disruption. Do not assume electricity, shops, ATMs, or mobile networks will remain available.

### Prepare the home

- Secure loose outdoor objects that could become airborne.
- Close and secure doors and windows.
- Keep away from glass and large windows when winds become severe.
- Move important documents, medicines, and electrical equipment away from likely floodwater.
- Charge phones and backup batteries before power interruptions occur.
- Keep drains and exits clear where it is safe to do so.
- Know how to turn off electricity and gas only if you can do so safely and have been instructed or trained appropriately.

Do not climb onto a roof or attempt dangerous repairs as the storm approaches.

## 4. Evacuation and Shelter

### When evacuation is ordered

An official evacuation order should be treated seriously, especially in low-lying coastal locations, flood-prone areas, unsafe buildings, and locations identified by local authorities.

1. Leave promptly using the advised route.
2. Take essential medicines, emergency supplies, identification, and necessary assistive devices.
3. Help children and anyone needing assistance without delaying dangerously.
4. Do not travel towards the coast to watch the storm or inspect the sea.
5. Do not cross flooded roads or attempt to drive through moving water.
6. Tell a trusted person where you are going, if communication is available.
7. Proceed to the designated shelter or other location directed by authorities.

Do not wait for water to reach your home before leaving an area under evacuation order.

### If you are sheltering indoors

- Stay inside a sturdy building away from windows and glass doors.
- Move to a safer interior room if conditions outside worsen.
- Avoid balconies, exposed rooftops, and rooms with large unsupported windows.
- Keep a torch and phone accessible.
- Follow official information and do not go outdoors to inspect damage.

A temporary reduction in wind may occur when the eye of a cyclone passes over an area. This does **not** mean the cyclone has ended. Dangerous winds can return from another direction. Remain sheltered until authorities confirm that it is safe.

## 5. Safety During Severe Weather

### Strong winds

Stay away from windows and exterior doors. Do not stand beneath trees, near unstable walls, beside signboards, or close to overhead electrical lines. Never attempt to retrieve objects blown outside during severe winds.

### Flooding and storm surge

Move to the safe location identified by local authorities. Avoid beaches, riverbanks, drains, underpasses, and low-lying roads during dangerous conditions.

Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater when you can avoid it. Water can conceal open drains, damaged roads, debris, and electrical hazards. Moving water can sweep people and vehicles away.

If trapped by rising water, contact emergency services if possible and move towards a safer location only when there is a clear, safe route. Do not enter floodwater to rescue someone; alert trained responders.

### Electricity and fire

Keep away from fallen power lines, damaged electrical equipment, and flooded areas where electrical wiring may be present. Never touch a fallen wire or attempt to move it with a stick or other object.

If you smell gas or suspect a leak, avoid flames and actions that could create a spark. Move away and contact the appropriate emergency or utility service from a safe location.

### Travelling

Avoid unnecessary journeys during a warning. If already travelling, follow official road closures and instructions. Do not use an overpass, bridge, or coastal route as a shelter unless authorities specifically direct you there.

## 6. What to Do After a Cyclone

The end of the strongest winds does not mean the area is safe. Floodwater, unstable structures, electrical hazards, contaminated water, and further rainfall can remain dangerous.

### First priorities

1. Check yourself and others for injuries.
2. Contact emergency services for urgent medical or life-threatening situations.
3. Stay away from damaged buildings, unstable walls, and hanging debris.
4. Avoid all fallen electrical wires and report them to the relevant utility or authorities.
5. Do not enter floodwater or use flood-affected electrical appliances.
6. Drink safe water and follow local public-health advice.
7. Use official information to confirm whether roads, shelters, and neighbourhoods are safe.
8. Return to evacuated locations only after official clearance or instructions from the responsible authorities.

### Drinking water and hygiene

Floodwater may contain sewage, chemicals, waste, or other contaminants. Do not assume water is safe because it looks clear.

Use sealed bottled water or water treated according to current public-health guidance. Follow local instructions for boiling, disinfection, and storage. Keep food and drinking-water containers away from floodwater.

### Damaged buildings

Do not enter a building with major structural damage, a leaning wall, a damaged roof, exposed wiring, or a suspected gas leak. Report the hazard and wait for appropriate assessment.

### Emotional wellbeing

Stress, fear, confusion, and exhaustion are common after a disaster. Help children and vulnerable people find a safe place, maintain contact with trusted family members, and seek medical or psychosocial support when needed.

## 7. Special Considerations

### Children

Explain the family plan in simple language. Keep essential medicines, identification, and contact details accessible. Teach children to go with a trusted caregiver and not to approach floodwater or fallen wires.

### Older adults and people with disabilities

Arrange transport, medication, mobility aids, communication assistance, and accessible shelter in advance. Do not assume that every shelter or evacuation route is accessible.

### Fishing communities and coastal workers

Follow official marine warnings and restrictions. Do not go to sea during unsafe conditions. Secure boats and equipment only when it is safe to do so, and do not return to the water until authorities permit it.

### Farmers and livestock owners

Follow local advice for livestock movement and agricultural assets. Do not risk human life attempting to save equipment, crops, or animals during dangerous weather. Avoid flooded fields and damaged electrical infrastructure.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Waiting until the last moment to evacuate.
- Trusting forwarded messages instead of official warnings.
- Going to the beach to watch high waves or storm surge.
- Standing beside windows during strong winds.
- Assuming a calm period means the cyclone is over.
- Driving or walking through floodwater.
- Touching fallen electrical wires.
- Entering damaged buildings before they are declared safe.
- Returning to an evacuated area because neighbours have returned.
- Using contaminated water without following public-health guidance.

## 9. Quick Cyclone Readiness Checklist

Before cyclone season or when a warning is issued, confirm the following:

- [ ] I know where to check official warnings.
- [ ] My household knows the evacuation route and shelter location.
- [ ] Essential medicines and emergency supplies are ready.
- [ ] Phones and power banks are charged.
- [ ] Important documents are protected from water.
- [ ] Children and people needing assistance have a support plan.
- [ ] Loose outdoor items are secured if conditions permit.
- [ ] I know to stay away from windows during severe winds.
- [ ] I will never cross floodwater or touch fallen wires.
- [ ] I will wait for official guidance before returning after evacuation.

## 10. Knowledge Review

A learner should be able to explain:

1. The difference between strong winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surge.
2. Why official warnings should guide evacuation decisions.
3. What to include in a household emergency kit.
4. Why people must remain sheltered during a temporary calm.
5. Why floodwater and fallen power lines are dangerous.
6. What safety checks are necessary before returning after a cyclone.

**Safety principle:** Protect human life first. Follow official warnings, evacuate when instructed, avoid dangerous water and electrical hazards, and return only when authorities indicate it is safe.
