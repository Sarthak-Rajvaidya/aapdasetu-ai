# Earthquake Safety

## Before an earthquake
- Identify safe spots in each room: under sturdy furniture, against an
  interior wall away from windows and tall furniture that could tip over.
- Secure heavy furniture, shelves, and water heaters to walls so they
  cannot topple.
- Keep an emergency kit accessible (see the general emergency-kit guide).
- Practice "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" with your household.

## During an earthquake
- **Drop** to your hands and knees so you don't get knocked over.
- **Cover** your head and neck with your arms, and get under a sturdy desk
  or table if one is nearby.
- **Hold On** to your shelter until the shaking stops; if there is no
  table nearby, hold on to your head and neck instead.
- Stay away from windows, mirrors, and anything that could shatter or
  fall.
- Do not use elevators. Do not run outside or between rooms while the
  ground is still shaking.
- If you are outdoors, move to an open area away from buildings, trees,
  and power lines.
- If you are driving, pull over away from bridges, overpasses, and
  buildings, and stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on until shaking
  stops.

## Immediately after
- Check yourself and others nearby for injuries before moving.
- Expect aftershocks; be ready to Drop, Cover, and Hold On again.
- Check for hazards: gas smells, damaged wiring, or structural cracks. If
  you smell gas, do not use switches or open flames — move away and
  report it.
- Evacuate calmly if instructed, using stairs, never elevators.
- Once outside, move to a clear, open assembly point away from damaged
  structures.

## After leaving the building
- Do not re-enter a damaged building until authorities confirm it is
  safe.
- Check in with family or your out-of-town contact if communication is
  available, but keep lines free for genuine emergencies.

This is general educational guidance based on widely published disaster
preparedness practice. In an active emergency, follow instructions from
local authorities and emergency services.
