# Tornado and Severe Wind Safety

## Watch vs warning
- A **tornado watch** means conditions favor tornado formation — prepare
  and stay alert.
- A **tornado warning** means a tornado has been spotted or indicated by
  radar — take shelter immediately.

## Identifying safe shelter
- The safest spot is a small interior room, closet, or hallway on the
  lowest floor of a sturdy building, away from windows and exterior
  walls.
- Basements, if available, are generally the safest option.
- Avoid garages, wide-span rooms (like gyms or auditoriums), and mobile
  homes, which offer little protection.
- If caught outdoors with no shelter available, get into a low-lying area
  such as a ditch, cover your head, and stay away from vehicles and trees.

## During the tornado
- Crouch low and cover your head and neck with your arms.
- Use a mattress, heavy blankets, or sturdy furniture for extra
  protection if available.
- Stay away from windows at all times, even if you can hear or see the
  tornado.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Do not try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle if it is very close, unless
  officially advised to relocate; seek sturdy shelter instead.
- Do not open windows — this does not equalize pressure and wastes
  valuable time.
- Do not assume a lull in wind means it's over; the calm area near the
  storm can be temporary.

## After the tornado passes
- Stay cautious of downed power lines, broken glass, and gas leaks.
- Wait for official confirmation before moving through debris-heavy
  areas.
- Check on neighbours if it is safe to do so.

This is general educational guidance. Always follow instructions from
local meteorological and disaster authorities during a real tornado
event.
