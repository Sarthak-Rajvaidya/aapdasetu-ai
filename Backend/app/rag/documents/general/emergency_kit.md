# Building a Household Emergency Kit

Every household should keep a basic emergency kit ready before any disaster
strikes, since utilities and shops may be unavailable for hours or days
afterward.

## Core supplies
- Drinking water: enough for at least three days per person (roughly 3-4
  litres per person per day for drinking and basic hygiene).
- Non-perishable food that needs no cooking (dry snacks, packaged food,
  glucose, energy bars) for at least three days.
- A battery or hand-crank powered flashlight and a portable radio, with
  spare batteries.
- A basic first-aid kit, plus any personal medication for at least a week.
- A whistle to signal for help if trapped or in distress.
- Copies of important documents (ID, insurance, medical records) kept in a
  waterproof pouch, plus a small amount of cash.
- A power bank and charging cables for phones.
- Face masks and hand sanitizer.
- Sturdy shoes and a change of clothes per person.
- A list of emergency contact numbers written on paper, not just saved on a
  phone that may lose charge.

## Special considerations
- For infants: extra formula, diapers, and baby-specific supplies.
- For elderly or disabled family members: mobility aids, hearing aid
  batteries, and any assistive devices they depend on daily.
- For pets: food, water, and a leash or carrier.

## Maintenance
Check the kit every few months: replace expired food and medicine, and
update the water supply. Keep the kit somewhere easy to grab quickly, such
as near the main exit.

This is general educational guidance and does not replace instructions from
local disaster management authorities.
