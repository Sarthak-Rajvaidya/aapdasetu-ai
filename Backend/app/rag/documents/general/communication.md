# Emergency Communication Basics

Communication often becomes difficult right after a disaster because
networks get congested or damaged. Planning ahead makes a real difference.

## Before a disaster
- Agree on an out-of-town contact that all family members can call or text
  if local lines are jammed — long-distance calls sometimes get through
  when local ones don't.
- Save emergency numbers (local disaster helpline, police, fire, ambulance)
  in every family member's phone and also write them on paper.
- Identify a family meeting point in case people get separated and cannot
  communicate.

## During a disaster
- Prefer text messages or messaging apps over voice calls — they use less
  network capacity and are more likely to get through.
- Keep calls brief so lines stay open for people with genuine emergencies.
- If official alerts are available through a phone app or radio, keep that
  channel active so you keep receiving updates.

## Do not
- Do not spread unverified information about the disaster; it can cause
  panic or misdirect help.
- Do not rely on a single communication method — have a backup plan such
  as a battery-powered radio.
