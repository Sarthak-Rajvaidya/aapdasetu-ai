Cyclone Preparedness

Coastal India safety — warnings, evacuation, and post-landfall hazards.

Course Progress0%

1. Overview & Learning Objectives

Tropical cyclones form over warm ocean waters and bring destructive winds, storm surge and heavy rainfall to coastal regions, including much of India's eastern and western coastlines.

  • Understand cyclone warning stages issued by meteorological authorities
  • Build an emergency kit appropriate for cyclone season
  • Know when and how to evacuate from coastal and low-lying areas
  • Recognize coastal flooding and storm-surge risk
  • Communicate safely during and after the storm

2. Warning Stages

Meteorological departments typically issue a sequence of alerts as a cyclone develops:

Watch

Cyclone conditions are possible — begin preparations and monitor updates.

Warning

Cyclone conditions are expected — finalize evacuation plans and follow official instructions.

3. The Eye of the Storm

Do not be misled by calm conditions. The "eye" of a cyclone can bring a temporary lull in wind and rain. Winds return suddenly from the opposite direction once the eye passes — stay in shelter until authorities confirm it is safe.

4. Evacuation & Coastal Flooding

Storm surge — a rise in sea level driven by the cyclone's winds — is one of the deadliest cyclone hazards for low-lying coastal communities.

  • Evacuate early if you are in a low-lying coastal area under a warning
  • Move to a designated cyclone shelter or safe area inland, not toward the beach
  • Do not wait to "see how bad it gets" once an evacuation order is issued

5. Emergency Kit for Cyclone Season

  • Battery or hand-crank radio for official updates
  • Drinking water and non-perishable food for 3+ days
  • Flashlight, batteries, first-aid kit
  • Important documents in a waterproof bag
  • Fully charged power bank / phone

6. After the Cyclone

  • Wait for an official all-clear before leaving shelter
  • Avoid downed power lines and standing water
  • Check on vulnerable neighbours once it's safe to do so
